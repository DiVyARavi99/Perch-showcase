Book I • Volume Sequence 02

Chapter 2: Copper Manifolds

“The valves do not forgive the salt; they only postpone its crystallization.”

At dawn, the glass ribs of the greenhouse groaned against the morning frost. Maya adjusted her wool sleeves and gripped the bronze petcock of the desalination feeder. Inside the subterranean culvert, three fathoms below the winter waterline of the estuary, the brine pumps were wheezing like old draft horses.

The copper manifold was weeping green verdigris where the sea salt had chewed through the leaden washers. Maya tapped the gauge with a fingernail wrapped in vulcanized rubber. Forty pounds of hydrostatic head, and the tidal gate had yet to close for low water. ! If the tidal surge spilled over the baffle before the heat coils reached temperature, the winter crop of salt-hardened chicory would drown in cold mud before the bell struck noon.

⁂

“You’re late with the charcoal slag,” she said without looking back toward the bulkhead stairs.

Julian didn’t answer right away. He dropped his wooden hod onto the floorboards with a dull wooden thud, showering dry oak embers across the damp cedar duckboards. His apron smelled of sulfur and elderberry wine. When he pulled his gloves free, his fingers were stained with walnut oil—the sign that he had been polishing the optical lenses in the south observation dome rather than tending the boiler flues.

“The fog came over the dunes before five,” he murmured, his breath forming small silver plumes in the chilly nave. “The lighthouse keeper claims the channel ice is already four inches thick south of the sand spit. The mail skiff turned back.”

Maya knelt beside the distribution valve. The brass was cold enough to bite through her denims. With both hands, she braced her heel against the brick pilaster and heaved against the cast-iron wheel until the oxidized threads sheared loose with a ringing shriek that startled the swallows roosting in the rafters.
