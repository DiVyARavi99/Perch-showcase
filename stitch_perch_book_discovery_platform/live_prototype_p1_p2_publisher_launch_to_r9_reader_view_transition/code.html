The Saltwater Greenhouse • Chapter 2

Copper Manifolds

By Maya Lin • Imprint: Riverhead & Birch Press

At dawn, the glass ribs of the greenhouse groaned against the morning frost. Maya adjusted her wool sleeves and gripped the bronze petcock of the desalination feeder. Inside the subterranean culvert, three fathoms below the winter waterline of the estuary, the brine pumps were wheezing like old draft horses.

The copper manifold was weeping green verdigris where the sea salt had chewed through the leaden washers. Maya tapped the gauge with a fingernail wrapped in vulcanized rubber. Forty pounds of hydrostatic head, and the tidal gate had yet to close for low water. If the tidal surge spilled over the baffle before the heat coils reached temperature, the winter crop of salt-hardened chicory would drown in cold mud before the bell struck noon.
