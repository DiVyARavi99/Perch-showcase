Milestone Prompt #3

Janina's Astrological Determinism vs. Bureaucratic Indifference in Chapter 8

In this sequence, Tokarczuk frames Janina Duszejko not as an unreliable narrator in the clinical sense, but as an acute observer who has constructed an alternative moral cosmos because the existing municipal power structures offer zero sanctuary for the living. Notice how the police inspector dismisses her celestial calculations while simultaneously accepting baseless rumors.

“The human mind is quick to explain what it cannot comprehend by inventing reasons that flatter its prejudices.”

— Olga Tokarczuk, Drive Your Plow Over the Bones of the Dead, p. 164

How do you interpret the motif of William Blake’s proverbs recurring in this exact encounter? Is Tokarczuk suggesting that visionary poetics are the only defense against rural alienation?

Does the moral culpability shift between the forest scenes and the police station?

Look closely at how the deer tracks are described right after the discovery of the trap lines. Tokarczuk uses almost liturgical adjectives for the non-human forest, while the police station in Kłodzko smells purely of wet plaster and cold lard. It seems like the narrative refuses to grant human social hierarchy any ecological authority.

The translation choices of Antonia Lloyd-Jones in Chapter 9: English rhythm vs Polish syntax

Antonia Lloyd-Jones preserves Janina’s peculiar capitalized nouns (“Anger”, “Night”, “The Gray Person”). In the Polish original, this device mimics 18th-century Romantic typography. Does it come across as endearing eccentricities or a profound theological declaration in your reading?
