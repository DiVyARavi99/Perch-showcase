In a remote Polish village, eccentric recluse Janina Duszejko prefers the company of animals and William Blake’s poetry to humans. When local poachers and hunters turn up dead under bizarre circumstances, Janina involves herself in the investigation with a shocking theory that nature is taking its revenge. Tokarczuk deftly subversive mystery novel examines madness, justice, animism, and the hypocrisies of modern civilization through an unforgettable, subversive female protagonist.

“The soul of an animal is like a light bulb that burns with a quiet, steady glow... we commit murders every day and pretend it is nutrition.”

format_quote Underlined in graphite by 89 readers

This serves as Duszejko’s philosophical thesis statement for the remainder of the narrative. Tokarczuk roots her animism not in naive sentimentality, but in an unrelenting moral equivalency. Notice Antonia Lloyd-Jones’ deliberate translation of 'light bulb'—it anchors an ancient, almost pagan sanctity straight into modern domestic life.
