edit_note p. 142 · Klara and the Sun

“Do you believe in the human heart? I don’t mean simply the organ, obviously. Do you think there is such a thing? Something that makes each of us special and individual?”

Kazuo Ishiguro · 2021 Edition 32 readers underlinings

Ishiguro strips away scientific pretense to interrogate whether consciousness alone grants us soulhood. What stops me cold on every reread is Klara’s total absence of cynicism: she examines human fragility not as a defect to optimize, but as a sacred mystery worth quiet reverence.

Notice how Ishiguro uses the physical sun as both biological necessity and spiritual benefactor. It reads almost like modern animism wrapped in gentle machine perception.
